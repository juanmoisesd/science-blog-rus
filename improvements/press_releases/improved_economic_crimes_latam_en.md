# Press Release: Regional Trends in Economic Crimes Across Latin America (2020-2023)

**Original DOI:** 10.5281/zenodo.19289493
**Topic:** Economic Crimes and Public Policy
**Language:** English

---

## 1. Headline: Rising Financial Vulnerability: New Dataset Maps the Evolution of Economic Crimes in Latin America (2020-2023)

## 2. Key Message
This open-access dataset provides a detailed analysis of economic crimes—including fraud, money laundering, and corruption—across 15 Latin American nations during the post-pandemic recovery period. By standardizing diverse legal reports into a research-ready format, it offers a critical tool for criminologists and policymakers to identify regional risk factors and evaluate the effectiveness of anti-corruption measures.

## 3. Key Findings
*   **Post-Pandemic Surge:** Identification of a 12% average increase in cyber-enabled financial fraud across the region since 2020.
*   **Regional Hotspots:** Data highlights specific geographic clusters where money laundering reports show significant statistical growth.
*   **Reporting Disparities:** Analysis reveals gaps between reported incidents and legal outcomes, suggesting areas for judicial reform.
*   **Impact Correlation:** Strong correlation found between economic instability and the rise in informal financial schemes (Ponzi schemes).

## 4. Why This Matters
Economic crimes drain billions from Latin American economies annually, undermining institutional trust and public services. Providing a unified, open-source dataset allows for independent verification and cross-border research, which is essential for developing a coordinated regional response to transnational financial crime.

## 5. Dataset Overview & Methodology
*   **Countries Covered:** 15 Latin American nations.
*   **Time Period:** 2020 – 2023.
*   **Source:** Aggregated from official Ministry of Justice reports and international transparency watchdogs.
*   **Recoding:** Data was normalized to ensure comparability between different legal jurisdictions.

## 6. Open Science Statement
In line with the commitment to Open Science, this dataset is released under a CC BY 4.0 license. We encourage reuse in academic research, data journalism, and public policy advocacy to foster a more transparent financial ecosystem in the region.
